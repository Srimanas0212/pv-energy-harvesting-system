# 5. System Architecture and Integration

## 5.1 Introduction

With the PV model, ML-based MPPT control, charge pump, and LDO each detailed individually in earlier chapters, this chapter covers the full integration of these subsystems and their interactions, signal propagation, and end-to-end energy conversion.

## 5.2 Overall System Architecture

The proposed system is a multi-stage design converting input solar energy into regulated electrical energy. Main blocks:

1. Photovoltaic (PV) Cell
2. Machine-Learning-based MPPT Controller
3. Voltage Controlled Oscillator (VCO)
4. Charge Pump (Voltage Doubler)
5. Programmable Low Dropout Regulator (LDO)
6. Bandgap Reference (BGR) Circuit

Energy conversion proceeds sequentially: produce → optimize → boost → regulate.

## 5.3 Functional Interconnection of Blocks

See [the Block Diagram](../images/5.1-Block_Diagram.png) and for full integrated schematic (implemented in Cadence Virtuoso) [Integrated Solar Energy Harvesting System](../images/5.2-Complete_Schematic.png).

- The **PV cell** is the primary power source, producing voltage and current dependent on irradiance and temperature.
- The **MPPT controller** takes measured PV voltage/current and environmental parameters, predicts the maximum-power operating voltage, and generates a corresponding duty cycle.
- The duty cycle drives the **VCO**, producing high-frequency clock signals for the charge pump's switching process.
- The **charge pump** performs voltage boosting via capacitive switching; the resulting boosted voltage feeds both the LDO and the BGR.
- The **BGR** produces a temperature independent and supply independent reference voltage `Vref`, used by the LDO for regulation.
- The **programmable LDO** uses `Vref` to produce a fixed output voltage, irrespective of ripples in the boosted input.

## 5.4 Signal Flow Analysis

1. The solar cell produces signals (voltage and current).
2. The MPPT controller processes these signals.
3. The MPPT controller outputs a duty-cycle control signal.
4. The VCO converts the duty cycle into switching pulses.
5. The charge pump uses those pulses for voltage boosting.
6. The BGR uses the boosted voltage to generate a reference voltage (combining CTAT and PTAT components).
7. The boosted voltage also feeds the LDO, which generates a feedback voltage compared against `Vref`.
8. The LDO stabilizes the boosted voltage into the final regulated output.

This flow ensures proper coordination between control and power stages, enabling efficient energy harvesting and conversion.

## 5.5 Control and Power Interaction

- **MPPT controller** : decision-making unit.
- **VCO + charge pump** : energy processing units.
- **LDO** : regulation unit.

The duty cycle generated by the MPPT controller plays a crucial coordinating role between the two stages: environmental changes alter the duty cycle, which in turn affects energy transfer through the system.

## 5.6 Implementation Strategy

A behavioral/circuit-level hybrid approach was used:

- **PV model and MPPT controller** was implemented in **Verilog-A** (behavioral).
- **VCO** was modeled behaviorally for flexible control.
- **Charge pump, LDO, and BGR** were designed at the **transistor level**.

The entire system was modeled and simulated in the **Cadence Virtuoso** environment, combining both system-level dynamics and detailed circuit behavior.

## 5.7 Design Considerations

- Proper interfacing between analog and switching blocks
- Stability of the control loop under varying conditions
- Efficient voltage boosting with minimal losses
- Compatibility between charge pump output and LDO input
- Consistent regulation via a stable reference voltage
